# ADR-064: SOU scene composition — the backdrop is a room SHELL, the engine owns the furniture

**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Status:** Accepted
**Deciders:** akadmin (Supreme Leader), Szarik
**Project:** SOU (Star-Office-UI fork)
**Related:** ADR-060 (spatial redesign + art licensing, amended), ADR-061 (product definition + invariants)

## Context

The first generated backdrop (2026-10-15) was a **furnished** room — desks, server racks,
shelves painted into the image. The result, reported by the operator and confirmed by
inspection, was a doubled scene: painted desks underneath the engine's own furniture
sprites, agent avatars rendering as if *inside* a desk, mixed projection (painted
front-facing cabinets vs isometric sprites), two independent shadow systems, and a floor
whose tile grid terminated in abrupt painted patches.

The cause is a contract the upstream code states and we had ignored. Upstream's Gemini
route (`backend/app.py:875`, `_generate_rpg_background_to_webp`) never generates a room
freely — it **restyles a reference image while preserving its composition**:

```
"Use a top-down pixel room composition compatible with an office game scene.
 STRICTLY preserve the same room geometry, camera angle, wall/floor boundaries
 and major object placement as the provided reference image.
 Keep region layout stable (left work area, center lounge, right error area).
 Only change visual style/theme/material/lighting according to: <theme>.
 Do not add text or watermark. Retro 8-bit RPG style."
```

The "region layout" clause is the whole point: the engine's furniture is placed at
**fixed coordinates** that the backdrop's composition is expected to accommodate. Painting
furniture into the backdrop is not a stylistic choice — it is a collision with
hardcoded sprite positions.

Measured facts that constrain any redraw:

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Backdrop canvas | 1280×720, drawn whole (not tiled) |
| Camera | top-down 3/4 oblique — **not** top-down |
| Original wall colour | `#44618a` (cool blue-grey), floor `#5f91b9`→`#94c1e8` |
| Sprite-layer palette | warm: lamp `#ffea99`, wood `#c1a46b`, cream `#e2d3b4`, `#dacca0`, black outlines |
| Engine-placed sprites (hardcoded in `frontend/index.html`) | `desk_v2` (218,417), `coffee_machine` (659,397), `serverroom` (1021,142), `posters` (252,66), `sofa` (IDLE_SOFA_ANCHOR) |

Note the shell was **already** cool and the props are **already** warm in the shipped
build — that contrast is the intended look, not the defect. The defect was furniture and
shadows baked into the backdrop.

## Decision

1. **The generated backdrop supplies the room shell only**: walls, baseboard, floor,
   wall-mounted decoration. **No furniture, no rugs, no decals, no baked furniture
   shadows, and no text or logos on the floor.**
2. **The engine owns everything the agents interact with** — desks, tables, sofa, server
   rack, plants, cats, posters — placed by the existing hardcoded/anchored coordinates.
3. **The backdrop must leave the engine-managed zones as clear floor.** The zones (and
   why) are declared machine-readably in `profiles/scene_shell_office.json`, generated by
   `scripts/make_scene_shell.py` — not held in prose.
4. **One world palette across both layers**: cool blue-grey shell, warm wood/cream
   accents. Neither layer may introduce a third art direction.
5. **No img2img and no reference-image restyle of the shipped art** — invariant #10 /
   ADR-060 still forbids it. The upstream *method* is not reusable; the upstream
   *composition contract* is. We reproduce the geometry by declaring it (canvas, camera,
   regions, clear zones), never by tracing their pixels.
6. **Post-process per asset class — the backdrop is not a sprite.** Measured flat-neighbour
   rates: the ORIGINAL shipped backdrop is 6 % flat with ~19 000 colours (a smooth painted
   image, *not* pixel art), while the prop sprites run 26–54 % flat with hard dark outlines.
   The shipped look is therefore "smooth detailed room + crisp outlined props", and that
   contrast is intended. Consequence: a sprite-grade quantization (≈20 colours) on the
   backdrop makes it *flatter than everything in the scene* (measured 70 % flat) and reads as
   banded posterisation. The backdrop quantises lightly (96 colours) and keeps its shading;
   sprites quantise hard. Both numbers live in `profiles/scene_shell_office.json`.

   Corollary for anyone repeating this: do not "fix" a perceived style mismatch by
   quantising harder without measuring first — that instinct was wrong here, and the
   measurement is two lines of numpy (`script: make_scene_shell.py --from-raw` recomposites
   without a GPU run).

## Consequences

**Positive.** The doubling defect cannot recur silently: a backdrop with furniture on the
floor is now a contract violation, and the clear zones are checkable against the
hardcoded sprite coordinates. The measured palette table means a future session does not
re-derive the two art directions by eye.

**Negative / costs.** The backdrop must be regenerated whenever the engine's furniture
coordinates move — the shell is coupled to the placement layer. That coupling is
deliberate: it is exactly the coupling upstream encoded in the "preserve major object
placement" clause, and it is cheaper than a backdrop that fights the sprites.

**Known seams at the time of writing** (engine-side, not asset-side): `hermes-szarik`
overlaps the desk instead of standing in front of it; the `cats` sprite sits at rack
height with no floor contact; avatars and props have no contact shadows.
